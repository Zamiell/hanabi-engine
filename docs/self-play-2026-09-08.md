# Self-play investigation: September 8, 2026

This is a diagnostic run, not the 200-game baseline. Seeds 1–28 were exercised
while fixes were being developed; their results must not be combined into a
single-version strength measurement. No expert actions or expectations changed.
The removed inverse-planning assumptions and scoring exemptions remain removed.

## Fixes

1. **Playable 5 Save admission:** a giver-visible playable 5 does not eliminate
   the recipient's unplayable 5 Save alternatives. Preserve Save admission when
   those alternatives remain, while retaining the productive Play interpretation
   when they do not.
   [Clue interpretation](https://hanabi.github.io/beginner/clue-interpretation/#clue-interpretation-algorithm).
2. **Chop protection versus identity accounting:** an unclued chop-moved card
   does not establish a public identity claim on its visible face. Previously
   this made observers disagree about a Trash Chop Move and the new chop.
   [Trash Chop Move](https://hanabi.github.io/level-4/#the-trash-chop-move-tcm).
3. **Priority timing:** capture the played card's existing obligation before
   advancing connections. Completing a blind play must not retroactively become
   an ordinary Priority choice.
   [Priority](https://hanabi.github.io/level-25/#the-priority-prompt--the-priority-finesse).
4. **Unnecessary-move identity leakage:** a signal naming pushed/ignited/moved
   cards must not attach the identity of the different card just played. That
   false fact eventually produced contradictory purple-4 requirements.
   [Unnecessary moves](https://hanabi.github.io/level-24/).
5. **Fix admission safety:** the general Fix-candidate path now runs the same
   recipient-safety validation as explicit required fixes. A repair obligation
   does not prove that a proposed clue actually repairs the recipient's note.
   [Fix Clues](https://hanabi.github.io/level-3/#the-fix-clue).

Recorded prefixes for seeds 6, 16, 18, and 22 reproduce these bugs. Their tests
assert admission/consistency invariants, not that the recordings are optimal.

## Convention question: seed p4v0s28, turn 1

[Open the recorded game at turn 1](https://hanab.live/shared-replay-json/415uqfppgbcqftogikrf-psnhwdhaakxnlecsmuuk-valxmbvrwijyd,03xdkd-wapaeaehel,0#1).
The subsequent actions are unvalidated engine moves, not an expert fixture.

Alice chooses **4s to Donald** as a 4 Charm on Bob's fourth Finesse Position
(b1). Alice cannot see her own hand. Bob can see Alice's newest-to-oldest hand:
**b1, y1, b2, p1**. The threshold helper treats her reachable, layered y1 as an
external connector, reducing Bob's required blind plays from three to two.
Consequently Bob does not recognize the Charm and clues yellow instead.

The [4 Charm rule](https://hanabi.github.io/level-23/#the-4-charm) establishes
the three-blind-play threshold. The
[extra examples](https://hanabi.github.io/extras/charms/#interaction-between-the-4-charm-and-double-bluffs-where-cathy-has-a-one-away-from-playable-card)
also show that another player's connector can invalidate a Charm. They do not
explicitly settle this unqueued, layered connector in the clue giver's hand.

**Question:** should Bob count Alice's y1 behind b1 as reducing the threshold
here, or does this remain a 4 Charm because that layered line is not already
established? If it does reduce the threshold, what should Bob's actual response
be, and what makes the original clue safe for Alice to give without knowing her
hand? No exception has been added for this case.

The projector already records a conditional hidden-connector dependency and
stops the chosen forecast after the clue; it does not prove Bob's blind play.
Nevertheless the heuristic ranking prefers the Charm. We should settle the
interpretation before choosing a policy for that conditional opportunity.
